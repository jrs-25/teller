# AI Leverage Log

I used Claude Code (Claude Opus, in VS Code) as a design partner and builder, and Claude on the web for a quick check of which ticketing platform Bankrate's public help center appears to use. Before building, I had Claude turn the brief into a working constitution ([CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md)): what the exercise rewards, a priority order, and a "build test" that defaults every feature to stub, describe, or hand off unless it proves a risky part. It also told Claude to call out scope creep and push back rather than agree. I worked the RFC left to right through the real system before writing code, with Claude proposing each decision and me accepting, changing, or overriding it. The RFC then served as the build spec: its safety rules became the test names. Claude also stress-tested its own work: it broke each safety mechanism on purpose to prove the CI test catches it, and it caught an error of its own (a compliance threshold of 0.0 that would have sent nearly every ticket to Compliance).

Where I overrode or redirected it mattered most. Claude first treated the ticketing system as a box on a diagram; I pushed on how Teller actually receives and writes to a live system people use all day, which produced the "beside, not in front" design and its biggest safety point (a public comment emails the member, so the integration itself can't post one). I rejected Claude's proposal to watch follow-up messages as a slippery slope of edge cases, and we replaced it with one rule: only write to the version of the ticket Teller read. I cut a seven-tag reporting scheme to one tag. From experience with a similar knowledge base for claims processors, I knew ingestion is a project of its own and that articles go stale, which led to wrapping the existing search and adding stale-content safeguards Claude hadn't proposed. Claude had quietly dropped the member profile service from v1; I sensed something was missing, and the gap was that it's the path to resolving account issues and so to the 80% goal, so the RFC now states that cost. When I asked for a flag to enable auto-replies, Claude argued it should require a code change; we settled on a reviewed config change behind three locks that compliance can never open. And when Claude's example of a gray compliance case turned out to be ambiguous on inspection, I made "where is the compliance line" the RFC's top open decision, owned by Compliance & Legal rather than engineering.
